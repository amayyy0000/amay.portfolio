Chapter 1 · Description of Data

Introduction

Descriptive statistics deals with organizing, summarizing, and presenting data to make information meaningful and easy to understand.

Types of Data

  • Qualitative (Categorical): Nominal, Ordinal
  • Quantitative: Discrete, Continuous
  • Binary: Only two outcomes (Yes/No)
  • Time Series: Data collected over time
  • Cross-Sectional: Data collected at one time

Frequency Distribution

Example of grouped data:

10–19 : 4 20–29 : 7 30–39 : 9 40–49 : 5

Measures of Central Tendency

  • Mean: Mean = (Sum of observations) / n
  • Median: Middle value of ordered data
  • Mode: Most frequent value

Example: For 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 → Mean = 6, Median = 6, Mode = none

Measures of Dispersion

  • Range: Maximum − Minimum
  • Variance (Sample):
s² = Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)
  • Standard Deviation:
s = √s²

Example: If variance = 16, standard deviation = 4

Shape of Distribution

  • Symmetric: Mean = Median = Mode
  • Positively Skewed: Mean > Median
  • Negatively Skewed: Mean < Median

Correlation

Correlation measures the strength and direction of linear relationship between two variables.

−1 ≤ r ≤ +1

Graphical Representation

  • Histogram – Frequency distribution
  • Ogive – Cumulative frequency
  • Box Plot – Spread and outliers
  • Scatter Plot – Relationship between variables
  • Pie Chart – Categorical proportions
